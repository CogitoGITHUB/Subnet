* What it is
# the project, its language, its health, and who maintains it.
  PRoot is a user-space implementation of chroot, mount --bind and
  binfmt_misc built on ptrace — no root, no setup (proot-me.github.io,
  fetched 2026-10-16). The Termux fork (github.com/termux/proot) is a copy
  of the project "with patches applied to work better under Termux".
  proot-distro builds rootless Linux containers from OCI images on top of it
  (its README, fetched 2026-10-16). On Android 12+ a separate mechanism
  interferes: the phantom-process killer, which kills background child
  processes beyond a small limit — commonly reported as 32 across all apps,
  death by signal 9 (github.com/termux/termux-app issue 2366 and Google
  issue tracker 205156966, read 2026-10-16; not tested on this device).

* What transfers
# one line per mechanism worth borrowing, each with the reason it fits a program here.
  - ptrace syscall translation is the reason a rootless Linux userland
    exists on this phone; tools we build must live within it — small
    binaries, no root, no privileged assumptions.
  - Prefer the Termux fork of proot for anything we run here — it exists
    precisely because upstream misbehaves on Android.
  - Treat process lifetime as unreliable: the phantom killer reaps
    background children. State that matters must be on disk and resumable —
    the same honesty herdr applies to its sessions.
  - One long-running foreground session, not many daemons, is the shape
    this platform rewards.

* What to avoid
# one line per mechanism we deliberately do not take, with the reason. No code.
  - Always-on background agents as a requirement: Android may kill them;
    design sessions, not daemons.
  - Trusting every syscall to translate: ptys, ioctls and watches each need
    their own verification under proot.

#+TITLE: Risks — AIU NOTATION
# One job: what can sink this program, the first sign of each, and what shrinks it
# Status: filled 2026-10-16; plans stay general, not yet reviewed.

* Risks
# one line per risk: what it is and how much it can hurt.
  - A mark that collides with prose or tags — rule 12 names this as the
    failure the design exists to avoid; it would sink everyday use.
  - Syntax creep: symbols for ordinary things returning — the goal dies
    with the rule (rule 12).
  - Reparse latency on the phone — typing feels heavy; incremental
    reparse is table stakes (research).
  - Literate tangling complexity — targets and dependency scanning grow
    edge cases; Org Babel's history is the warning.
  - The mark choice stalls — the sign and its typing comfort are picked
    by the phone test inside item 2 (D-014); until a sign types cleanly,
    the first build step waits.
  - Wording drift: "no engine" and "no format unless necessary" read
    against the engine surface the contract needs — decisions block
    before architecture.
  - Tier-3 pressure — math and graphs pulling effort early, against
    rule 12's far-future stance.
  - The phone cannot prove the graphics tier — rendering ambitions may
    wait for the laptop.

* First signs
# the earliest observable signal that a risk is happening.
  - A mark typed as a collision by accident; a symbol sneaks back into an
    ordinary element; typing lag; target and scanning cases multiply; the
    mark choice stalls; the engine/format wording blocks a decision.

* Shrinking it
# what is done about each risk, or why nothing can be.
  - Choose the mark with the owner on the phone and test it against real
    prose and tags before anything is built (rule 12).
  - Keep the smallest slice — everyday tier first; refuse tier-3 work
    until the owner opens it.
  - Treat incremental reparse and error recovery as the first slice's
    work, not later polish.
  - The engine/format wording is settled (D-012) before architecture
    begins.
  - Record every phone limit in WHEN-ON-LAPTOP.org the moment it
    appears.
